# Catalog of meaning failures -- 12 modes

Read during AUDIT (step 5 of the SKILL.md protocol). Mandatory minimum run -- modes 1, 3, 4, 5. The rest -- by symptom (the "symptom -> test" table at the bottom of this file).

Each mode: name / symptom / procedural test (input -> operation -> criterion) / short example.

---

## 1. Pseudo-depth

**Symptom:** a phrase sounds significant, but substituting its opposite does not break it.

**Test (antonym test, BSR):**
- Input: a contested claim.
- Operation: substitute the antonym of the key term.
- Criterion: did the phrase become false, or does it sound just as "deep" as the original? Still just as deep -> empty.

**Example:** "Attention is the currency of the gaze" -> the antonym "inattention is the currency of the gaze" sounds just as significant -> test fails, the claim is empty.

---

## 2. Jargon nominalization (content-free abstraction)

**Symptom:** an abstract phrasing that nobody dares to unpack into a concrete statement.

**Test (two-sentence test):**
- Input: an abstract phrase.
- Operation: unpack it into a checkable statement in two sentences.
- Criterion: cannot be unpacked in two sentences -> jargon, not a thought.

**Example:** "the work explores the boundaries of the visible" -- does not unpack in two sentences into a checkable statement (what exactly is visible, where is the boundary, how does the work show it) -> fails.

---

## 3. Hermetic drift

**Symptom:** a reading holds together not because of elements in the work, but because the author/critic wants to see it that way.

**Test (Eco, falsifiability):**
- Input: a meaning hypothesis about the work.
- Operation: name an element of the material that would refute it.
- Criterion: cannot name one -> a conjecture, not intent.

**Limit of the test:** on a short form (slogan, post) there isn't enough of a "whole" for the test to apply cleanly; it degrades there.

**Example:** "it subverts habitual modes of contemplation" -- which element of the work would refute this? If there is no answer, the hypothesis is unfalsifiable, not intent.

---

## 4. Slop

**Symptom:** nobody commissioned the text/concept and the author is not ready to stand behind it, or the effort is disproportionate to the result.

**Test (Willison + Kommers, three axes):**
- Input: a finished artifact.
- Operation: (1) did someone ask for this? (2) did I read it and am I ready to stand behind it? (3) is the effort comparable to handmade work -- would it blend into a stream of same-shaped generation?
- Criterion: both Willison questions "no" -> slop regardless of quality. A meaningful concept only saves axis (3) -- competence; it does not fix the asymmetry of effort or the scalability.

**Example:** a generated post made "for appearances," which the author would not reread as a reader -- it is slop even if formally meaningful.

---

## 5. Thesis-shaped cliche (reward-hacking the map)

**Symptom:** the Meaning Map is formally filled in every field, but the claim is the mode of a distribution, not a find.

**Test (group homogenization + antonym test on the claim):**
- Input: a filled-in map.
- Operation: (1) would any other author, with the same tool, on the same input, get roughly the same map? (2) run the claim itself through the antonym test (mode 1).
- Criterion: the map matches what anyone else would get -> a mode of the distribution, not a find. Homogenization is a **group-level**, not an individual-level, effect -- comparing "does this look like my other variants" does not catch it; you need "does this look like what anyone else with this tool would get." Both checks are conjunctive: a claim stays in the map only if it passes BOTH the group test and the antonym test; failing either -> reject (the two tests catch different diseases: mode-of-distribution and emptiness).

**Example:** "art forces us to slow down in an accelerating world" -- a generic claim any author with any generator could reach about almost any topic on modernity -> fails the group test.

---

## 6. Curse of knowledge

**Symptom:** the author does not notice the audience lacks knowledge that the author considers obvious.

**Test (competence inventory):**
- Input: a key figure/reference/term in the work.
- Operation: write out what specific knowledge the viewer needs to read it.
- Criterion: the knowledge is non-trivial and not given anywhere in the work itself -> curse of knowledge, not successful density.

**Example:** a reference legible only within the author's narrow professional circle, with no fallback channel for anyone else -- the risk was not accounted for.

Note: the effect is confirmed quantitatively in the literature (Birch & Bloom 2007, d=0.469/0.645, N=155; Ryskin & Brown-Schmidt 2014, d=0.20/0.24, N=3074 -- replication did not fully confirm the effect).

---

## 7. Clinamen alibi

**Symptom:** a deviation from the work's own stated rule is presented as a device, when in fact it was a failure, explained after the fact as intentional.

**Test (clinamen vs. alibi):**
- Input: a point of deviation from a stated rule/pattern.
- Operation: (1) was literal compliance with the rule physically possible at this point? (2) was the deviation planned in advance as part of the intent, or introduced after the fact?
- Criterion: compliance was possible but not followed, and the explanation appeared after the fact -> alibi, not a device.

**Example:** a series with a "deliberately broken rhythm" -- the break was noticed after editing, and the explanation "this is an ironic disruption" appeared only in conversation with the author.

---

## 8. Silent transfer failure

**Symptom:** a metaphor or pun carrying the key meaning does not land, and the audience gives no signal about it.

**Test (fallback channel):**
- Input: a figure (metaphor/pun) carrying key meaning.
- Operation: check whether this meaning has a fallback channel (rhyme, antithesis, paratext) that will still work if the figure does not land.
- Criterion: the only channel is the figure, and it is a metaphor/pun (not a rhyme/antithesis) -> the meaning can fail silently: the audience will not say "I didn't get it," it will simply walk past.

**Example:** the key idea of an exhibit rests only on a pun in the title; if the visitor does not catch it, the show communicates nothing in its place.

Note: metaphor and pun fail without a signal; rhyme and antithesis survive a lack of cultural competence (McQuarrie & Mick 1999).

---

## 9. Reading divergence

**Symptom:** the viewer understood the work differently than the author intended, and it is unclear whether the form should be fixed.

**Test (two-layer audit, Eco/Hall, plus a fourth outcome):**
- Input: an unexpected reading.
- Operation: (1) *Eco layer* -- name the elements of the piece the reading rests on; if it does not rest on any, it is use, not interpretation, and the dispute is closed. (2) if it does rest on elements -> *Hall layer*: did the viewer understand and reject it (oppositional), or do they have a different code (aberrant)? (3) separately check a fourth outcome -- non-engagement (never reached them, no prompt, wrong viewing context) -- this is not a subtype of misunderstanding.
- Criterion: oppositional -- not fixable by form (it's the viewer's position); aberrant -- fixable by form (clarify the code); non-engagement -- fixed by access/prompt/context, not by meaning.

**Example:** part of the audience read the work as a critique of technology, though the author intended tenderness toward it -- if this reading rests on real elements of the work, the question is not "fix the text" but "did the viewer reject the position, or is this a different code."

---

## 10. Mixing levels of revision

**Symptom:** words get edited while the unresolved problem sits one level up (the material, the composition, the event itself).

**Test (level of revision, Schmid):**
- Input: a spot that needs revision.
- Operation: name which of four levels the problem sits on -- event (Geschehen) / story (Geschichte) / narrative discourse (Erzaehlung) / presentation (Praesentation, the words) -- BEFORE changing the wording.
- Criterion: the problem sits on levels 1-3, but only level 4 (words) gets revised -> the fix is cosmetic, the problem remains.

**Example:** the ending of a text "doesn't work" -- the last sentence gets rewritten, though the real cause is that the story itself (level 2) does not supply material for a strong ending.

---

## 11. Cancellable implicature

**Symptom:** a key implicit claim can be cancelled with "...but I don't mean that" without feeling contradictory -- meaning it was never actually conveyed.

**Test (cancellability, Grice):**
- Input: an implicit claim p that the author believes the text implies.
- Operation: say/add "...but I don't mean p."
- Criterion: it sounds contradictory -> p is part of the literal content, conveyed. It sounds fine, no contradiction -> p is an implicature and it is fragile: either state it explicitly, or knowingly accept the risk that it will not land.

**Example:** a post implies "algorithms are harmful," but the sentence "...though I don't think algorithms are harmful" does not contradict the text -> this claim was not conveyed, it only seems that way to the author.

---

## 12. Glued-on meaning

**Symptom:** the meaning is declared in a caption/wall text, but not synthesized by the form -- it rests on one channel only.

**Test (synthesis, Cook):**
- Input: a stated meaning + the artifact (image/sound/text/exhibition context).
- Operation: swap one component (the picture, the music, the viewing situation) for another.
- Criterion: the message does not change -> the meaning rests on one channel and is glued on top, no synthesis.

**Example:** replace the background music in a video with something neutral -- if the stated meaning "anxiety of the digital age" survives untouched, it was never embodied in the form, only stated.
