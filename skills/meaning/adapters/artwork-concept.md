# Adapter: artwork concept / series / artist statement

## Six things to keep separate

| What | Question |
|---|---|
| Topic | What field does the work sit in? |
| Artistic question | What question does the work ask (without a ready answer)? |
| Author's thesis | What does the author propose to see differently (= `claim`)? |
| Mechanism of the work | What does the work concretely DO (process, device, rule)? |
| Viewer's experience | What does the viewer live through, not merely "understand"? |
| Context | What conversation (cultural, technological) does the work enter? |

A "topic" without a mechanism is not a concept. Always demand the mechanism.

## Multiplicity vs. vagueness

- **Supported multiplicity:** several readings, each grounded in concrete elements of the work. For art, this is a value.
- **Vagueness:** a phrasing so general that any reading sticks to it. Diagnosis: if a reading cannot be tied to an element of the work, it is not supported.

Do not collapse multiplicity into a single reading; check `counter_reading` -- which reading is undesirable and what in the work provokes it.

## Layer-6 test -- mandatory

Does the form embody the thesis, or illustrate it?

- Embodies: regenerated family photos where the faces lose recognizability -> the form itself is about the fragility of memory.
- Museum label: a pretty picture + a wall text "about the fragility of memory" -> the meaning is glued on from outside.

Test question: **if you remove the wall text, does the thesis remain in the work itself, at least as a tension?**

## Artist statement (TRANSLATE)

Skeleton: observation (concrete) -> mechanism (what the work does) -> tension -> what is proposed to be seen differently -> why now. No phrasings from `memory/rejected-cliches.md`; every abstraction must rest on a mechanism.
