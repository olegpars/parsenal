# Adapter: open-call application

## Two maps, not one

```
A. Internal meaning of the work (Meaning Map of the work as it is, with no regard for the open call)
B. An honest link to the open call's context
```

Do not repaint the work to fit a curatorial text: if A has to be bent to fit B, an honest "this work is not for this open call" is worth more than a forced application.

## Map B -- the real intersection

- Which question raised by the open call is **already present** in the author's practice (check `memory/recurring-questions.md`)?
- Which part of the work answers it -- a concrete element/mechanism, not a topic?
- What exactly is proposed to be made/shown (deliverable)?
- Why should this exist **in this specific institution/context** (site, audience, program)?
- What will the open call's viewer get out of it?
- What separates the idea from "just a fitting phrasing" -- where is the mechanism, not a coincidence of words?

Mark any point without an honest answer as `empty` -- this is a signal of a weak application; the author decides.

## Checks (AUDIT of the application)

- Does the application just paraphrase the curatorial text back at the curators? (red flag)
- Is the thesis of the work visible, or only the topic of the open call?
- Are the promises deliverable (format, timeline, tech rider)? Flag what is not deliverable, do not verify it yourself.
- Phrasings from `memory/rejected-cliches.md` -- replace, or back them with a mechanism.

## TRANSLATE output format

Concept note: title -> the work in one paragraph (observation + mechanism + tension) -> link to the open call's question (map B) -> what exactly will be shown/made -> why here and now -> what the viewer will get.

## Win-odds profile

Not a taste judgment ("like/don't like") but an honest read of position within the competitive field. Built after RESEARCH (if it ran) and before the final assembly of the application.

1. **Competitive audit** -- from `research-notes` (RESEARCH, step b): which quadrant of the field this application occupies, which positions are already taken by other likely applicants.
2. **Check against explicit brief criteria** -- line by line against the selection-criteria list from `research-notes` (RESEARCH, step a): the application meets / does not meet / partially meets each one.
3. **Verdict** -- in one line: "odds improved by X (a concrete position/mechanism/empty quadrant) / lowered by Y (a concrete unmet criterion or taken position)." Do not build a verdict without RESEARCH -- mark it `empty` and note that a competitive audit is needed.

The verdict is not a prediction of the result but a diagnosis of position; the decision to submit or not stays with the author.
