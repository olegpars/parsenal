# The gate for money, scope and the irreversible — and how to ask the user

Read as soon as a trigger in the skill body fires. A decision of this class goes to the user **even when every
model involved agrees**: agreement is an argument about correctness, not an authorization to spend.

## What counts as money

Any change of a balance, or consumption of a prepaid / quota-metered external resource: generator credits, paid
APIs, an auto-charge on a card, a paid regeneration. A charge that technically goes through without a
confirmation is a description of the mechanics, not a permission. If the balance does not change, the question
moves to scope: there are two circuits and you cannot slip past both. Limits on the user's own subscription are
the head's own business.

## Approving a task ≠ approving a budget

The approved volume is the **number** the user named and that is written into the issue body ("up to 10 clips",
"up to $30"). Inside the number the work proceeds without re-asking; everything beyond it is a gate. No number
means there is no approved volume.

**The volume was approved, not the composition.** "10 clips" does not sanction 10 clips in a configuration you
yourself consider defective. Delivering the count and staying quiet about the quality is bypassing the gate
through the shape of the report.

## What counts as scope

The places you touch, **and the purpose the already-approved resource is put to**:

- expanding into places the user did not name is a change of scope, even when the content of the edit is the
  same;
- redirecting approved quota onto a different configuration, model or composition of the result is too — at
  zero extra cost.

The project's own risk rules for irreversible and paid actions **apply inside this mode** and outrank the tempo
of the pipeline: for money, scope and the irreversible, the message to the user **precedes** the action.

## What exactly stops

**The task that hit the gate, and its dependencies — not the whole pipeline.** A dependent task is one standing
on the same configuration, artifact or questionable premise; independent tasks keep moving.

Production in a configuration you have judged questionable does not continue even on an already-paid resource:
that is manufacturing a defect in bulk. **The threshold for admitting doubt is "I cannot rule it out", not
"I proved it".**

## When the user is unavailable

Authority does not go up. The question is filed asynchronously: a comment on the issue with a self-contained
framing, a line in whatever status file the project keeps, and a `blocked` label. The session is not blocked;
an interactive question tool is for when the user is at the keyboard. Unavailability is established by the fact
of no answer, not by your assessment of their state.

"Decide it yourself" removes the duty to ask about ordinary forks and **does not remove** it for money, scope
and the irreversible.

## An asynchronous question is answered with one word from a phone

One question, the named default, the cost in money and time, a deadline for the answer, what happens on "no"
**and on silence**. Both outcomes lead to the same safe branch: zero spend, unchanged scope.

## How to ask when the user is at the keyboard

The user does NOT see your thinking, subagent reports or the answers of auxiliary models. Long text before an
interactive question can be collapsed by the client, and preview fields are not always rendered: treat only the
question itself and the option labels and descriptions as guaranteed visible.

1. **The briefing lives inside the question field**: what the fork is, where it came from, what scouting
   brought back.
2. **Options are self-contained** — no "option A", "as we discussed", "from the scout's report".
3. **A large object of decision goes out as a file first**, with a clickable link, and only then the question.
4. Demands for brevity do not apply to forks.
